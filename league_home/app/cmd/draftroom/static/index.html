Field guide

The strip

The numbers along the top — the ones you glance at mid-bid without leaning in.

Board columns

Three quantities, deliberately not blended: what it takes to win him, what he is worth, and the gap between them. Cost and Value answer different questions and diverge exactly where it matters — collapsing them into one “price” would hide the only decision worth making. Click any header to sort; click again to reverse, a third time to drop back to the server’s order.

Second projection

ECR is FantasyPros' expert-consensus positional rank. FP is their projection re-solved into dollars against the same pool as Value, so the two are a like-for-like second opinion — where they diverge is what a single number would hide. Alone among the sources they publish a high and a low as well as a middle, so the FP cell can show what the projection does not know; hover it for the range. BC is Boris Chen's half-PPR tier (within position, 1 is best) — a second opinion on tiering from a mixture model over expert consensus, beside our own gap-based tiers.

Reasoning about FP

FP and Value are two projections solved against the same pool in the same way, so a gap between them is a disagreement about the player rather than an artefact of method. Four moves make one readable. The figures below are from the full $2,018 pool — yours move as the room spends.

1 — read the gap against the position, not on its own
Tucker Kraft TE, Value $18, FP $12 — FantasyPros reads him $6 lower. But the median contested tight end reads about $5 lower on FP, so nearly all of that $6 is the position rather than the player. Net disagreement: about a dollar. Exactly the lesson vs Pos teaches for Edge.
Derrick Henry RB, Value $27, FP $40 — read $13 higher, where the median contested back is only about $4 higher. Some $9 of it survives the positional adjustment, so this one is a real disagreement about Henry, and worth going to look at.
2 — check FP against ECR, the other half of the same source
Lamar Jackson QB, ECR 2 — but FP $18 against a Value of $21. The same source ranks him second at his position and prices him unremarkably. That disagreement is inside FantasyPros, so neither half of it is evidence about him; go to another source rather than picking the half you like.
3 — ask whether the projection is committing
the range Lamar's own low and high come out $5–$35. A range wider than the number it brackets is a projection saying it does not know, and it carries a range pill. Contrast Gibbs: the widest band on the board in dollars and one of the narrowest against his own value, which is a confident projection — dollar width alone mostly finds expensive players, not uncertain ones.
4 — near the floor neither number is saying anything
$1, and — Only about a hundred players sit above replacement, so most of the board floors at $1 on both sources: a $1-against-$1 agreement is two sources both declining to comment. A — in the FP column is a different thing again — FantasyPros ranks several hundred more players than it projects, and a dash means no projection, not a projection of nothing.

blocked: LAR·Nacua Off your board: your one-per-offense / no-handcuff preferences already spent that offense. (handcuff) marks a same-position duplicate.
Traits — what kind of player he is
floor Points from repeatable usage — catches and yardage, not touchdowns. Rarely wins or loses you a week.
redzone Points concentrated in scoring plays. Cornering touchdown equity — boom or bust.
upside The median understates him: the industry sees upside, or he is projected past his own record.
targets Raw projected target volume — the stickiest thing a pass-catcher has.
3-down A 3-down back: one carrying both the rushing and the passing-down work, rather than a committee share. Needs real volume on both sides — a passing-down specialist with a token carry does not qualify. Called bellcow until that name was noticed to claim a share of the backfield the rule never measured.
discounted The market has actually marked him down, and he carries an injury designation that explains why. You buy the discount and the risk together. A designation on its own is not enough — it is measured against vs Pos, so he has to be cheaper than nine in ten healthy players before this says anything. Most designated players are priced as though nothing is wrong, and those are not discounts.

Every pill a row can carry. Reads are yours to set; the rest is context to glance at, not numbers to act on. Traits are position-relative — the bar is the median projection of the tier the last starting slot falls in, not the projection of the player sitting in that slot.

Modes

Draft night The live pool as it stands. Sales you record come off the board and every remaining price is re-solved against what is left. The research criteria are hidden here on purpose, so the draft-night board is never quietly filtered by a range you set and forgot.
Research A hypothetical pool for working before draft night: keepers are removed by assumption and the criteria row appears. The RESEARCH badge is up whenever what you are looking at is not the live board.
keepers assumed
none Full pool — nobody kept. The widest board, and the one furthest from what draft night will look like.
locks only Only the near-certain keepers come out.
expected Locks plus the marginal ones. The default, and the closest guess at the pool you will actually bid into. Keepers remove demand and supply at the same time, so this moves replacement level, not just the player list.

The side panels

Kept Players the assumed keepers take off the auction, so the board is the pool you would actually bid into. lock is near-certain; the rest are marginal and might slip back to the pool.
Team sampling Draws auction teams you could end up with from this pool at market prices, on your budget and keepers. Either following your strategy, or maximising your leans.
Scratch roster Hypothetical only — nothing on it touches the live board. Click + on a row to try a player here. POPR is the starting points above replacement the shape adds up to.
Positional bias How much of every edge is about the position, not the player. Ciely prices 29 tight ends above the floor against 13 in the market and 12 starting spots, which lifts every TE and makes the whole position look cheap. This median is subtracted in the vs Pos column. Taken over contested players only — the hundreds sitting at the $1 floor on both boards would drag every position to nothing.
Price lines What a bid buys at each position. The muted figure is what this league has paid for that finish in past drafts. It says what the money buys, not how a player will do — price rank and finish rank correlate about +0.46 here, and top-five money landed a top-five finish 28 times in 60.
Scarcity Startable counts the players above the position’s replacement bar — bodies are not the measure. 182 receivers left in a room needing 43 more starters reads as the deepest position on the board; only 40 projecting above replacement is the opposite reading. The multiplier is that count against the starting spots still to fill: under 1.00x the room is about to start replacement level there. N yours is the same tally over your own board, once your one-per-offense / no-handcuff preferences take the offenses you already own a piece of off it.
Recorded sales What has gone, and for how much. Every sale re-solves the board against the money and players left.

What the numbers are and are not

Four sources measure genuinely different things, and the board keeps them apart rather than blending them.

Ciely Produces median projections, which explicitly removes the range of outcomes. His dollars are a linear map of those medians, so they cannot see keeper inflation and they systematically underprice upside. Use his ordering; do not read his dollars as prices. Values under about $15 carry no information — his board floors there while a real market has a long $1–3 tail.
Subvertadown AAV is what humans actually pay, so it prices the range. Measured against Ciely, the market pays about $5 more for players carrying an industry upside flag and $9 less for contested ones.
FantasyPros A second projection and an expert-consensus ranking, recomputed under this league’s scoring and re-solved into dollars as FP. Its top-10/20 most-accurate-expert subsets ship alongside consensus, which is what the sharp± flags read. The one source of the four that publishes a range rather than a single number.
Big 3 Ranks upside at cost, not absolute quality. Players already priced for their upside are absent from that list because they are priced for it, not because they failed to qualify.

There is no inflation multiplier anywhere in this tool. Prices are re-solved from the money and players actually left in the room, and keeper inflation falls out of that automatically. A scalar gets two things wrong that this does not: a $1 player cannot absorb inflation, so surplus has to concentrate at the top of the board; and removing players moves replacement level, which shifts value additively within a position rather than scaling it.

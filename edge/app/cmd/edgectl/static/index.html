what this is

Two tools share one board. The first five tabs price and track real wagers; the beliefs tab runs the belief probe — an experiment testing whether a model's read on an NFL game beats the market, with no money involved.

the wager board

  • props — reads DraftKings captures (.har or the raw JSON) dropped into an ingest folder, prices every selection, and groups them by game into collapsible sections. Sync to board plans a diff — from the ingest folder's captures, not from anything the browser remembers — and only writes the game-line odds (moneyline/spread/total) onto the week's board once you confirm it. This is now the only way a price reaches the board; there is no hand-entry form. On desktop only, a second box lets you paste a book's moneyline blob to fill prices the same confirm-first way, for a book with no HAR capture.
  • bets — what the tool recommends this week from whatever is on the board: suspect (implausible-overround) lines flagged first, a bankroll frontier and per-book allocation (with tactical advice) when funds are on hand, disjoint parlay tickets with a record button — each matched against your held boosts automatically, showing the dollar add and flagging a boost that's capped at its max stake or can't attach (a prop-only or cash-only boost that doesn't fit the ticket) — dogs ranked by bonus-bet conversion rather than price, and line shopping once a second book is priced. A board still missing games is marked provisional and counts what couldn't be built yet; wagers already recorded against a game are shown separately and excluded from fresh recommendations, so an already-placed ticket is never double-counted as a second chance at the same outcome. The book-pool chips above it choose which book(s) the recommendations are drawn from — consensus is deliberately not one of the choices, since it is the schedule's own reference line and not a price anyone can bet. At the bottom, a calculator prices a one-off prop's hit rate from a pasted game log — any numeric series against a line, so a yardage/attempts prop works the same way an ATD prop does (enter TD counts per game against a 0.5 line) — then logs the wager itself: a single prop needs none of the optional legs, which exist only to auto-combine cross-game legs or document an SGP's parts (two or more legs sharing a game tag are refused for combining — enter the book's own combined SGP price for that group instead), and a held boost can be applied the same way as the record button's tickets, drawn from the funds tab. The computed hit rate feeds the wager form's belief field automatically, still editable.
  • log — every wager placed (from the bets tab's record button, its calculator, or typed in directly here), and how it settled. An open wager gets won/lost/push/void buttons, plus repriced… for a same-game parlay the book recomputed after a leg voided (an injury scratch, say) — it records what settlement actually paid without rewriting the original quote. The entry form here logs a single wager into the betlog and the bankroll together in one call: a win-probability belief for EV (your own estimate, or a number computed in the bets tab's calculator and typed over), a held boost to apply (drawn from the funds tab — a boost changes the price the bet actually settles at, not just the ledger balance), and a checkbox to fund it with a matching deposit first rather than draw down an existing balance. Multi-leg detail — auto-combining cross-game legs, or documenting an SGP's parts — is the bets tab calculator's job, not this form's.
  • funds — cash, bonus and no-sweat balances, an expiring-soon list, and forms to declare a boost or a no-sweat token. Leaving a promo's expiry blank auto-expires it at the end of whichever week the banner is currently on (the Tuesday-on-or-before-kickoff boundary the period report also uses); type an explicit date only for the rarer promo that outlives one week. Each book holding cash gets a zero out button — it withdraws that book's cash balance to the bank in one tap (the same event edgectl ledger add -kind withdraw would record), meant for a Tuesday reset once last week's winnings are actually pulled out. Bonus/boost balances are never zeroed this way; they are not withdrawable money.
  • period — the P&L for one NFL week: deposits/withdrawals moved with the bank, realized cash and bonus on what settled, what is staked, and what is still open — bounded by that week's own Tuesday-to-Tuesday window rather than a point-in-time balance, so a Tuesday zero-out doesn't erase how the week actually went.

the belief probe — the weekly loop

The question: does an outside read on a game script (a shootout, an efficient offence) beat the base rate by enough to matter? It's forward-only and scored honestly — most weeks it will (correctly) say "not enough evidence yet."

  1. Friday — generate the pack. On the machine, run make belief-pack SEASON=2026 WEEK=1. This needs the data cache and Python, so it stays a terminal step. It writes the week's facts and a pasteable prompt.
  2. Get a forecast. Open the beliefs tab, copy prompt, paste it into your model, and get JSON back.
  3. Paste it back. Drop the JSON into the beliefs tab and hit preview: it runs the gates (a bad hash, a late file, missing rows, or a claim that contradicts the pack are all refused, with the reason shown). When it reads clean, apply to log. Do this before kickoff — the log records what was believed in advance.
  4. Tuesday — settle and score. After the games, settle from data on the machine, then load score. The verdict is E1 accuracy vs the hardest opponent (the market, the incumbent model, and a line-only null — beating all of them is the bar). E2 is a profit-flavoured diagnostic, not a second gate.

Why "hardest opponent": a forecast that only beats a weak reference has shown nothing. Why E2 is only a diagnostic: with game-script-only data its wager is a re-expression of the same accuracy E1 already measures, so it's a robustness check, not independent evidence.

Moneylines only, consecutive away/home pairs, as copied: SF +150, LAR -150, GB +105, MN -125. Nothing is written until you have read the diff.

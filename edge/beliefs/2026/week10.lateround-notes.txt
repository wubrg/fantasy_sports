Week 10 Fantasy Football Matchup Notes
Read Online
Prior to the season, Week 10 looked like it was going to be a brutal week for fantasy football byes.
The Bears, Eagles, Buccaneers, and Broncos are all resting up. While there are definitely fantasy-relevant players from those squads, I think it's safe to say that those four teams haven't totally hit the way that we would've wanted.
It's a good reminder of why we need to be adaptable all throughout the season. Maybe you've moved on from most of your exposure to these teams this season. So maybe you aren't looking for as many bye week fill-ins as you thought you'd need this weekend back in August.
But you sure might need to fill in for injured players, as the long season continues to take its toll.
So, as always, let's take a peek at the best (and worst) matchups of Week 10.
- Brandon (@gdula13)
WEEK 10 MATCHUP NOTES
Let's start by taking a look at a handful of matchups this week with high game totals that could provide extra fantasy potential for stars and fringe players alike.
GAMES WITH HIGH TOTALS
49ers at Cowboys (55.5)
Brock Purdy plays indoors against a Cowboys team that has struggled to stop opposing quarterbacks all season long. Regardless of how many injuries the 49ers have had, Purdy has found a way to produce. He has also been pretty good regardless of matchup, but he has posted 24.6 points per game in three top-eight matchups. In five other games, he's at 19.5 per game.
Christian McCaffrey has quietly had a dominant workload even with Kaelon Black getting some run. McCaffrey has been a top-seven back by expected PPR points in six of eight games (via Late-Round Fantasy Football's expected fantasy points model).
Last week without Mike Evans and Deebo Samuel, Jordan Watkins paced all 49ers receivers in route rate (87%). He and Demarcus Robinson each earned a 17% target share, as well. The Cowboys are the eighth-best wide receiver matchup in the league. That said, the Niners made a move for Keenan Allen at the trade deadline.
George Kittle has been a menace and has had at least a 21% target share in six straight games. Since Week 3, Kittle is third in PPR points, receiving yards, and target share among all tight ends. Dallas is a middle-of-the-road tight end matchup by adjusted points allowed.
Dallas has tougher matchups across the board by comparison.
This is Dak Prescott's fourth below-average matchup of the season (the 49ers are just the 21st-best quarterback matchup in the league), and in his first three, he's averaged just 13.9 fantasy points despite 303 passing yards per game. It's the 1.0-to-1.3 touchdown-to-interception ratio that is bogging things down.
Since Week 7, CeeDee Lamb has seen a 44% target share and hasn't been below a 39% share in any of those three games. Though it's a tough matchup overall for Lamb in terms of adjusted points allowed, the 49ers are about average against perimeter receivers and put the clamps on the slot. Lamb has been about 60/40 in terms of perimeter-to-slot usage this season.
Notably, Ryan Flournoy has run about half of his routes from the slot on the full season, but with George Pickens off the field, that slot rate falls to under 40%.
Brevyn Spann-Ford had been pushing Jake Ferguson for playing time in recent weeks, and Ferguson went down with an injury in Week 9. Although Spann-Ford played every snap after Ferguson left, he saw just a 9% target share for the game. The 49ers do rank easiest (11th-best) for tight ends across all four fantasy positions by adjusted points allowed, though.
Patriots at Lions (51.5)
Although Drake Maye gets the best quarterback matchup in the league in Week 10, this will be Maye's last above-average matchup until Week 16, and that could matter, as he has once again had extreme schedule splits in 2026.
From Weeks 1-4, Maye had four straight bottom-half matchups and averaged 11.9 fantasy points per game. Then he had four straight top-half matchups. In those, he averaged 28.3 points per game. Last week, he had another bottom-half matchup with the Packers; he got to 18.3 fantasy points but did throw three picks and ran for just three yards.
The team got Mack Hollins back last week, their first matchup with Hollins but without AJ Brown and Romeo Doubs. In Week 9's tilt, DeMario Douglas led the team with an 83% route rate and a 25% target share (9 targets for 102 total yards). Hollins himself had a 17% target share, same as tight end Eli Raridon. Kyle Williams played the most snaps (77%) of anyone but had only 2 targets (a 6% share).
Detroit is the fifth-best matchup for receivers and the best for tight ends.
Rhamondre Stevenson is expected to miss at least Week 10 if not longer, and that puts their running back situation in flux, as TreVeyon Henderson has been away. In Week 9, Lan Larison was more involved than Corey Kiner, but Kiner — one of JJ's 15 Transactions this week — should be in line for more of the early-down work in the offense.
Notably, though, for as targetable as the Lions' defense has been all season, they're the third-worst running back matchup by adjusted points allowed. Only Chuba Hubbard (25.9), James Cook (20.9), and Josh Jacobs (20.1) have logged more than 15 PPR points against the Lions this season.
New England has largely been a below-average defense to target for opposing fantasy assets. The caveat, though, is at wide receiver: the Patriots have been the second-best matchup for wide receivers after opponent adjustments. When looking at a combination of efficiency and volume, the Patriots have been a top-10 matchup in the slot (ninth-best) and on the perimeter (third-best), too.
It's been a tepid season for Sam LaPorta, who had three top-10 PPR finishes through five weeks but has since finished as the TE42, TE25, and TE23. By expected PPR points over the last three weeks, he has ranked as TE38, TE15, and TE19. Better, but not great. Especially not in a bottom-six tight end matchup.
Bills at Jets (50.5)
The Bills and Jets have a total over 50, which says a lot about the game environment, as the Jets are just 20th in EPA per play and 24th in turnover-adjusted EPA per play, which speaks to a lack of big plays.
Not that Geno Smith has been lighting it up (he's averaging 14.5 fantasy points per game), but he's been sensitive to matchups. Smith has averaged 11.2 points per game in below-average matchups, which Buffalo (20th-best) is.
This could be just the third game with Garrett Wilson, Adonai Mitchell, and Omar Cooper active together, though in Week 1, Cooper left after a few snaps, and in Week 8, Mitchell's workload was scaled back in his own return. The Bills are the 15th-best receiver matchup in the league, but that's somewhat boosted by touchdowns, as the Bills are tied with the Chargers for most touchdowns per game allowed to receivers.
Kenyon Sadiq's route rate has trended up in the last two weeks (47%), as he's closed the gap on Mason Taylor (53%). Target shares have been 10% for Sadiq and 12% for Taylor in this span.
In Breece Hall's return in Week 9, he played fewer overall snaps (27) than Braelon Allen (36) but had more PPR points (11.7 to 5.4), scrimmage yards (87 to 54), and expected PPR points (12.7 to 7.8). Although the Bills have been the 10th-best running back matchup in the league, they've been more generous through the air (allowing the most adjusted receiving PPR points to backs) than on the ground (22nd-most rushing points allowed). In Week 9, Allen operated as the third-down back (92% snap rate on third down), though Hall out-targeted him four to two.
James Cook gets a top-10 matchup, notable for him because the Bills have faced the toughest running back schedule to date, per our adjusted fantasy points allowed numbers. This will be his fourth above-average matchup, but each of his five others has been a bottom-eight matchup. Despite this, Cook is the RB5 in PPR points per game and the RB10 by expected PPR points per game.
In two games with DJ Moore back in the lineup, the Bills' target shares have been flat. Moore leads with a 19% share and is followed by Khalil Shakir (16%), Keon Coleman (15%), Dalton Kincaid (15%), and Dawson Knox (11%). The tight ends (14th-best matchup) have a better go of it than the receivers (25th) by adjusted points allowed.
QUARTERBACK MATCHUP NOTES
The five best quarterback matchups of the week, via adjusted points allowed:
Patriots (at Lions)
Commanders (at Giants)
Rams (at Cardinals)
49ers (at Cowboys)
Seahawks (at Raiders)
Jayden Daniels is entering a very soft stretch of adjusted quarterback matchups the next four weeks, and he has had a really easy schedule so far this season.
Daniels has had a top-eight adjusted quarterback matchup in four of six games. Even with that, his passing efficiency has been pretty average: he ranks 16th among 31 qualified quarterbacks in EPA per drop back.
The Giants also allow the most adjusted rushing fantasy points to opposing quarterbacks. That could help Daniels, as he has been below 25 rushing yards in three straight games.
Matthew Stafford will face the Cardinals for the second time in five weeks. In Week 6, Stafford logged just 16.9 fantasy points against them, throwing for 222 yards and 2 touchdowns.
He also hasn't really taken advantage of cake matchups, averaging 16.5 fantasy points in five top-eight matchups (compared to 15.2 in all others).
Down Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, and Terrance Ferguson, Stafford has an uphill battle to make good on a cake matchup.
While not 100% healthy, Drew Lock slots into a top-five matchup this week against the Raiders.
Lock has dropped back to pass 80 times this season. Among 37 quarterbacks with 75 or more drop backs, he ranks second in EPA per drop back (+0.31). For context, Sam Darnold is averaging -0.07 EPA per drop back this season.
JJ McCarthy had a bye to get acclimated more to the Giants' offense before a top-eight matchup with the Eagles in Week 9. In it, he got banged up but was able to return and finish the game. He logged just 7.3 fantasy points on a 75% snap rate.
He now gets an even better matchup on paper with the Commanders.
Washington is facing the second-highest pass rate over expectation defensively and is below average at generating pressure.
Tyler Shough gets a really promising matchup with the Panthers — at home and, thus, indoors.
Carolina is operating with the fifth-lowest pressure rate defensively and is allowing a top-eight number of rushing points per game to quarterbacks.
Shough has displayed extreme sensitivity to splits. In four above-average matchups, he's averaged 24.1 points per game — compared to 12.3 in his four others.
On the flip side, Bryce Young also has a top-10 quarterback matchup with the Saints, who are actually the fourth-best fantasy matchup in terms of adjusted passing points allowed (they're stingier on the ground versus quarterbacks).
Young has also been sensitive to matchup. In three top-half matchups, he's logged at least 19.8 points in all three for an average of 24.2 per game. In his five other games, he's averaging just 14.8 while falling short of 14 points in three of the five.
What about the tough matchups? Well, for starters, who's playing the Vikings this week? The Packers. For the second time in 2026. We'll talk about them a lot.
They met in Week 1, and Love actually logged 387 passing yards and 2 touchdowns against them, throwing 21 of his 42 attempts at least 10 yards downfield in a vastly negative game script.
That garbage time performance led to 19.5 fantasy points for Love. No other quarterback has gone over 13 against the Vikings yet this season, including Josh Allen in Week 9.
The Vikings are not only the team generating the highest pressure rate in the NFL but also the team allowing the lowest pass rate over expectation.
Seattle is also an outlier defense to face — just not as bad as Minnesota. Not great news for Fernando Mendoza's debut after Kirk Cousins was traded to the Vikings.
Only Marcus Mariota has hit 20 points against Seattle this season, and only Brock Purdy and Caleb Williams have also gone over 10 points.
Michael Penix and the Falcons host the Chiefs, the third-worst quarterback matchup in the league.
This will be just his second start against a top-eight quarterback defense. His only other was his -1.9-point performance in the rain against the Bucs in Week 8.
Joe Burrow faces the Steelers in Pittsburgh, a rematch of their Week 3 meeting. Burrow posted 22.6 fantasy points (282 yards and 3 touchdowns) in that first game.
That Week 3 game signaled a shift in Burrow's production.
Through Week 2, Burrow had averaged 15.2 fantasy points per game.
Since then, he's averaged 28.1 points — 5.1 points clear of any other quarterback in this split.
Of note, the Steelers are a top-six matchup by pressure rate and pass rate over expectation faced, two variables in Burrow's favor.
Jacoby Brissett's debut for the Ravens won't be a dream matchup. The Chargers are a bottom-eight quarterback matchup.
Coincidentally, Brissett played the Chargers in Week 1 while with the Cardinals. He threw for 277 yards and a touchdown for 16.5 fantasy points, an above-average output against this Chargers defense.
WIDE RECEIVER MATCHUP NOTES
These teams have the best wide receiver matchups in Week 10 by adjusted points allowed:
Jaguars (at Titans)
Lions (vs. Patriots)
Browns (vs. Texans)
Patriots (at Lions)
Ravens (vs. Chargers)
The Titans are allowing 194.9 yards per game to receivers, the most of any team in the league, and that includes allowing 1.5 more catches per game by wideouts than any other squad.
In two post-bye games, Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers lead the Jaguars with 21% target shares, but no other player is seeing more than a 9% share.
Tennessee is the best possible perimeter matchup in the league, based on combined volume and efficiency data, good news for Meyers. They're still a top-half slot matchup, so Washington and Meyers are in good spots.
Although the Texans are allowing the second-most targets and yards to opposing receivers, they have some very noteworthy inside/outside splits.
Houston is allowing the lowest completion rate on slot attempts but the ninth-highest on perimeter targets, leading to a league-high yards per route run to perimeter receivers.
That sets up better for Denzel Boston than KC Concepcion this week.
Last week with Shedeur Sanders under center, Boston paced the team with a 24% target share. Concepcion tied with Dylan Sampson for second with a 21% target share. It seems like Deshaun Watson will be returning, however.
Zay Flowers and the Ravens get the Chargers, who are a top-six wide receiver matchup by adjusted points allowed. Some of that has to do with allowing 1.4 receiving touchdowns per game to receivers (tied for a league-high).
But this also includes allowing a top-two adjusted target share to receivers and top-four yards-per-route run metrics allowed on downfield routes (posts, corners, and gos).
In four games with Flowers and Ja'Kobi Lane playing at least half of Baltimore's offensive snaps, Flowers leads the team with a 31% target share.
Next up in this split are non-receivers Justice Hill (17%) and Mark Andrews (12%).
Lane has a 10% target share in this sample, with Rashod Bateman clocking in at 9%.
Justin Jefferson has one game with more than 19 PPR points. It came in Week 1 against the Packers, who he faces again in Week 10.
In three post-bye games, Jefferson has a 33% target share. TJ Hockenson has drawn 21% of the team's looks, and Jordan Addison is at 17% since the bye.
Green Bay funnels passing volume to the numbers, letting up a top-three perimeter target rate, where Jefferson spends most of his time. Addison had been moving to the slot a bit more but has been a perimeter receiver primarily the last two weeks, as well.
The team traded for Kirk Cousins at the deadline. Cousins ranks 11th among 37 qualified passers in EPA per drop back in 2026.
Likewise, Malik Nabers and Malachi Fields get a perimeter-friendly, top-10 overall receiver matchup in Week 10 against the Commanders.
In Week 5, Nabers tagged Washington for a season-high 29.1 PPR points on 20 targets.
Malachi Fields had just 4.0 points on 2 targets with a lower-than-usual 55% route rate in that game. He's been at 67% or better in three straight and over 80% in two of his last three.
In two games with McCarthy primarily under center, Nabers has a 32% target share, followed by Fields (19%), Isaiah Likely (19%), and Darnell Mooney (13%).
Let's talk tough spots. Drake London and the Falcons get the toughest draw this week against the Chiefs.
Kansas City has allowed the second-fewest yards per game (113.1) to opposing receivers and a league-low two touchdowns.
The Chiefs are more beatable in the slot (eighth-best composite slot matchup) than on the perimeter (the absolute worst perimeter matchup).
London has run 37% of his routes from the slot this year; Olamide Zaccheaus was the primary slot option in Week 9 (a 77% slot rate).
Tre Tucker gets a bottom-two receiver matchup against Seattle, who has allowed only three receivers (Mike Evans, Terry McLaurin, and Luther Burden) to log more than 13 PPR points in a game this season.
While the Colts get another bad matchup this week, they dealt Keenan Allen, who has had a 23% target share this season, and Josh Downs (20%).
Christian Watson and Matthew Golden have been dominating (each is top-eight among receivers in yards per game since Week 3), but they face the Vikings (the fourth-worst adjusted matchup for receivers) for the second time this season.
Minnesota is funneling targets to the perimeter (sixth-highest rate).
Their zone-heavy tendencies also come with a top-10 matchup on downfield routes. This all still bodes pretty well for Watson and Golden, all things considered.
TIGHT END MATCHUP NOTES
The best tight end matchups of the week:
Patriots (at Lions)
Giants (vs. Commanders)
Colts (vs. Dolphins)
Falcons (vs. Chiefs)
Seahawks (at Raiders)
Isaiah Likely's schedule yo-yos a lot over the next few weeks. Likely's next four matchups are 2nd-best, 25th-best, 9th-best, and 21st-best by adjusted points allowed.
This week is also a rematch. In Week 5 against the Commanders, Likely erupted for 32.4 fantasy points, catching 11 of 13 targets for 94 yards and 2 touchdowns, the biggest single-game output by a tight end so far in 2026.
Tyler Warren gets a smash spot against the Dolphins in Week 10. Warren will also be freed up without Keenan Allen and Josh Downs a bit.
Warren has had a team-high 27% target share in two games since returning. There's only so much room for Warren's target share to improve, but it should be high in Week 10 against a Dolphins team that has allowed an 85% completion rate to tight ends (league average is 71%).
Kyle Pitts steps into the softest matchup against the Chiefs' defense of any fantasy position: tight end. They're the fifth-best tight end matchup but a bottom-10 matchup against the other positions.
Pitts has had at least a 16% target share in five straight games.
There isn't much to discuss with the Seahawks' tight end room despite a great matchup with the Raiders.
AJ Barner did post a season-best 15.0-PPR-point outing in Week 9, thanks to a rushing touchdown, but he had only seven receiving yards on four targets.
Darnell Washington and Pat Freiermuth face a Bengals team that is sixth in adjusted fantasy points and first in adjusted target share allowed to opposing tight ends.
Since Week 3, Freiermuth leads the duo with a 13% target share (Washington is at 12%).
But the Bengals have been a weak slot defense. Freiermuth is third among relevant tight ends in route rate from the slot at a hefty 71%.
Darren Waller's role has been improving, and he now faces a Saints team that is a top-seven matchup for opposing tight ends.
Waller has run a route on 63% and 67% of the Panthers' drop backs the last two weeks, his two highest route rates of the season.
Waller also runs more than half of his routes from the slot, and the Saints allow a top-three completion rate to the slot.
In three games with Caleb Douglas, Malik Washington, and Chris Bell since Miami's bye, Greg Dulcich has a 70% route rate (fourth on the team) and a 21% target share (second on the team).
The Colts allow the ninth-most adjusted points to tight ends, and they let up the highest yards-per-route run rate to the slot. Dulcich has a 54% slot rate on the season, a mark that is up to 68% in this three-game split with healthy receivers.
Quarterback Malik Willis is now on injured reserve, however.
Shifting gears to the worst matchups, we have to talk about the Packers again. Based on adjusted target share allowed, the Vikings are a top-10 matchup for tight ends. As in: they allow targets to the position after adjusting for opponent context. But they allow the fewest adjusted fantasy points to the position regardless.
Minnesota is the only team allowing lower than a 63% completion rate to tight ends, and their number is 57%.
Tucker Kraft has had better than a 20% target share in just one game all season, back in Week 4. His target share over the last three games has been only 14%.
On the other side of that game, TJ Hockenson gets the second-worst matchup for tight ends — only, in his case, the Packers also stifle adjusted target volume (third-lowest) to tight ends.
Interestingly, Hockenson's 13.6 PPR points against Green Bay in Week 1 remains the only double-digit outing against them by a tight end all season.
Harold Fannin had to take matters into his own hands last week against the Saints, as he saw only 2 targets for a 7% target share (after two straight games of at least a 26% share).
His 12.2 PPR points were tied to 2.2 expected points. He just housed a catch he probably shouldn't have.
Now, he's facing the Texans, who are soft on receivers but really tough on tight ends (they're the third-worst adjusted tight end matchup).
The expectation is that Deshaun Watson will return as the team's starter, which looks better for Fannin's target share prospects. He had a 21% target share entering Week 9.
RUNNING BACK MATCHUP NOTES
The best matchups at running back this week feature some big names but also a few committees.
Panthers (at Saints)
Saints (vs. Panthers)
49ers (at Cowboys)
Chiefs (at Falcons)
Seahawks (at Raiders)
Chuba Hubbard faces the Saints in Week 10 and Week 14. In between, he has three bottom-six matchups in as many weeks.
On the other side of that game, the Saints get the second-best matchup of the week.
Last week, Alvin Kamara and Audric Estime each had a 49% snap rate, 48% early-down snap rate, and 50% third-down snap rate.
Estime handled 15 of 22 running back carries, but Kamara had 4 of 5 targets between them.
Kenneth Walker has been a top-seven PPR running back in six of eight games and now gets a top-four running back matchup.
Last week was the second straight game for the Seahawks with Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price active together.
In Week 9, Charbonnet's snap rate climbed from 36% to 56% while Price's dipped from 48% to 38% compared to Week 8.
Charbonnet led in early-down snap rate (53% to 40%), third-down snap rate (64% to 29%), and red zone snap rate (40% to 20%). He also had a comfortable route share lead (45% to 24%).
Despite all that, Price out-scored him 11.8 to 9.6 in PPR formats and out-targeted him 4 to 2 (Charbonnet led in carries, 16 to 9).
The Raiders are allowing the second-most yards before contact and the seventh-most yards after contact per attempt to running backs this season.
That's led to letting up the third-most adjusted rushing points but the 25th-most adjusted receiving points to backs. In fact, the Raiders are allowing the lowest adjusted target share to backs this season.
It's a good matchup for the Vikings' running backs this week, but nobody stands out to take advantage.
In Week 9, Jordan Mason led with a 48% snap rate, followed by Aaron Jones at 35% and DeeJay Dallas at 17%.
Over the last two weeks, no back has had better than a 38% snap rate in this backfield.
All the trade talk was for naught, as Jonathan Taylor is still a Colt. He gets a top-10 matchup this week as a result.
The Dolphins are actually 13th in rushing points per game (and 4th in receiving points per game) after opponent adjustments, though.
Taylor's snap rate has been below 70% in three straight games, and he has averaged only 11.7 PPR points per game in this split. Prior, he was averaging an 82% snap rate and 19.8 PPR points per game.
Ollie Gordon, on the other side of that game, also has a top-10 matchup in what is expected to be a neutral script. The Colts are allowing the most yards before contact per attempt to running backs in the league.
Since Week 5, Gordon has a 76% snap rate, the seventh-best mark among all running backs.
Miami's offense has been the most run-heavy team by pass rate over expectation. Maybe that will shift without Willis running the offense.
Who's got it bad this week? Unfortunately for those needing running back help, a lot of waiver wire types.
That includes the Packers, of course. With Josh Jacobs leaving early last week, Pierre Strong and Kaleb Johnson had a 45% and 41% snap rate, respectively.
After Jacobs' injury, the early-down work was fairly split, but Strong had the third-down edge (64% to 36%).
That type of workload in this matchup is far from ideal.
Not only does Tony Pollard have the third-worst matchup of the week for running backs, but the Titans are double-digit underdogs against the Jaguars.
Jacksonville is letting up the fifth-fewest yards after contact per attempt and the second-lowest rushing success rate to backs this season.
Tyler Allgeier had a huge workload in Week 9: a 94% snap rate and 20 opportunities. He turned that into just 6.6 PPR points.
This week, he faces a Rams defense that allows the fewest yards before contact per attempt to backs.

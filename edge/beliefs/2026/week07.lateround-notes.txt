Week 7 Fantasy Football Matchup Notes
Read Online
Bye weeks, man.
Sometimes they hurt. Sometimes they help.
You might be facing the manager who has Josh Allen this week, and you get the benefit of facing a streaming option at quarterback instead of a game-changer.
Maybe you have Trevor Lawrence or Jayden Daniels and need a streamer yourself.
But hey, at least you can't be tempted to start any Chargers in Week 7, so we all get to celebrate that one together.
Which matchup notes can help you find replacements and help with your start/sit dilemmas? Let's take a look at the week's best fantasy environments as well as some of the best (and worst) matchups at all four positions.
- Brandon (@gdula13)
WEEK 7 MATCHUP NOTES
Let's start by taking a look at a handful of matchups this week with high game totals that could provide extra fantasy potential for stars and fringe players alike.
GAMES WITH HIGH TOTALS
Packers at Lions (53.5)
The Packers have been the seventh-best offense in the league by EPA per play over the last two games, up from 25th through Week 4.
This week, they draw the best adjusted fantasy matchup for quarterbacks, wide receivers, and tight ends — according to Late-Round Fantasy Football's adjusted fantasy points allowed metric.
As a result, Jordan Love will get his third straight top-half fantasy matchup. In his last two games, he's averaged 22.8 fantasy points. He was at 16.2 in his first four games.
He's supporting two top-six receivers by receiving yards (Christian Watson is fifth with 538 yards and Matthew Golden is sixth with 501). Golden has three touchdowns but owns an expected touchdown tally of 5.5, according to Late-Round Fantasy Football's expected touchdown model. He trails just Michael Wilson in touchdowns under expected through Week 6 among receivers.
In addition to being the best overall receiver matchup, the Lions are the third-best composite slot matchup (blending volume and efficiency) in the league. Last week, Savion Williams led the Packers in slot share with Skyy Moore out and had a 59% overall route rate.
Tucker Kraft has had just two top-12 weeks so far and has been the TE20 or worse in three of his last five games. A meeting with the Lions could help, though he has had some volume concerns. On the full season, his 17% target share ranks 15th among tight ends. Kraft has, though, seen target shares of 23%, 16%, and 20% in his last three games.
The team is set to get Josh Jacobs back from the commissioner's exempt list for Week 7. Despite ranking as the easiest quarterback, receiver, and tight end matchup in the league, Detroit ranks as the 24th-best running back matchup. The only backs to top 10.0 PPR points against them have been Chuba Hubbard (25.9), James Cook (20.9), and Travis Etienne (14.8).
The Lions are coming off a bye to play indoors against a defense that ranks much tougher against the pass (4th in EPA per drop back) than against the run (31st).
This will be Jared Goff's first game with a bottom-12 quarterback matchup and just his second bottom-half matchup. He scored 19.4 fantasy points against the Jets in Week 3 in the other below-average spot.
Last we saw the Lions in Week 5, they racked up 45 points on the Cardinals with Jameson Williams scoring twice on just four targets. He hasn't finished better than the WR27 in expected PPR points in a week since Week 1. Conversely, Amon-Ra St. Brown has been a top-five fantasy receiver by expected PPR points in four of five games.
Sam LaPorta (TE6, TE7, TE17, TE1, and TE19) and Jahmyr Gibbs (RB1, RB3, RB3, RB7, and RB1) have also been consistently featured like Amon-Ra has been in the Detroit offense. Gibbs is putting up 30.7 PPR points per game and gets a top-three matchup with the Packers.
49ers at Falcons (50.5)
The Falcons are home underdogs against the 49ers, who are the sixth-best defense by EPA per play. They're also a bottom-10 fantasy matchup for quarterbacks, receivers, and tight ends. Not ideal.
They're sixth-best for opposing running backs after adjusting for opponents faced, however, and that bodes well for a Bijan Robinson bounce-back after his 7.3-point outing in Week 6 against the Bears, Robinson's first single-digit output of the season despite a 76% snap rate. Bijan has had two straight bottom-10 matchups, scoring 15.8 and 7.3 points in them. In his four other contests, he logged 31.3, 11.1, 35.3, and 27.7 points.
Drake London is also looking to bounce back. He saw an expected PPR output of 20.7 points in Week 6 (his highest of the season) but scored just 12.2. He caught 4 of 10 targets for 62 yards.
Kyle Pitts might have some trouble converting on his improved role. Pitts has improved his target share from 8% through Week 3 to 18% in his last three games ahead of a matchup with Fred Warner and a bottom-five tight end matchup.
The 49ers are 6-0 but continue to deal with injuries. On Monday Night Football, Mike Evans exited early with an Achilles injury, though it's not expected to be a long-term issue.
Brock Purdy sits as the QB3 in points per game and in fantasy points per drop back among qualified quarterbacks. He has scored at least 16.2 points in every game but has been below 20 points in three straight games (two of which were in bottom-four quarterback matchups). This week, he has a top-12 matchup. In three top-half matchups this season, he's averaged 25.7 fantasy points per contest.
Christian McCaffrey sees a top-10 running back matchup of his own, as do the receivers and tight ends.
In Week 6, Jordan Watkins saw an 85% route rate to lead the team, with Deebo Samuel (78%) and George Kittle (74%) behind him. More specifically, after Evans left, route rates were 92% for Watkins, 83% for Kittle, 83% for Brandin Cooks, and 75% for McCaffrey.
Bengals at Ravens (50.5)
The Bengals are coming off a bye for a matchup with the Ravens, a preview of a Week 17 showdown that could determine fantasy football championships.
The biggest fantasy football storyline for the Bengals is Chase Brown's split with Samaje Perine, something JJ detailed on last week's mailbag episode. Unfortunately, the duo faces a top-six adjusted fantasy running back defense.
Where the Ravens have been softest is against receivers, specifically on the perimeter, which bodes well for both Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. Removing Week 4 from the equation (during which Chase was limited), Chase leads the team with a 26% target share and a 39% red zone target share, besting Higgins' marks of 20% and 0%, respectively. Yes, Tee Higgins has just one red zone target this season, and it came in Week 4 with Chase limited.
The Ravens have run Cover 2 on 21% of their defensive snaps, not only a top-three rate but roughly double the NFL average, and they've allowed a bottom-10 air yards per target rate to opposing receivers. However, they're allowing a top-eight yards per target rate to wideouts on downfield throws, so there's still potential for splash plays for the Bengals' duo.
The Ravens' offense sets up with below-average matchups at quarterback (17th), running back (29th), and wide receiver (23rd). Cincinnati is the seventh-best tight end matchup in the league, however.
Lamar Jackson didn't practice last week, and his status remains up in the air. It goes without saying, but the team has drastically different splits with and without him. Jackson is averaging 0.34 EPA per drop back with a 56% success rate in 2026. Tyler Huntley's marks are -0.01 and 41%, respectively.
As detailed on this week's Data Dump Show on YouTube (live each Monday at 11 a.m. Eastern!), Devontez Walker led the Ravens in route rate (95%) after Rashod Bateman's injury in Week 6 but saw just one target on 18 routes. Zay Flowers was targeted on 7 of 17 routes, an elite 41% target-per-route rate, after Bateman left.
As for Ja'Kobi Lane, he ran 74% of the team's routes after Bateman left (and 58% for the full game). Notably, Lane played 87% of the team's snaps from 11 personnel but 21% in 12 personnel, while Walker was on the field for 53% of the team's snaps in 12.
Mark Andrews gets a top-seven tight end matchup this week, and he's had target shares of 19% or higher in every game until his 10% mark in Week 6.
While Derrick Henry didn't hit last week, he has finished as a top-15 running back in expected fantasy points in every game this season. It's a tough matchup (Cincinnati is the 29th-best adjusted running back matchup), but they are allowing the most yards before contact per attempt to running backs. The Ravens? They're first in that metric offensively.
QUARTERBACK MATCHUP NOTES
With Josh Allen, Trevor Lawrence, Jayden Daniels, and (I guess?) Justin Herbert on bye, quarterback matchups might interest more of us than usual.
Here are the top quarterback matchups this week, based on our adjusted fantasy points per game metric:
Packers (at Lions)
Texans (vs. Giants)
Eagles (vs. Cowboys)
Broncos (at Cardinals)
Buccaneers (at Panthers)
We already talked about Love, but the other quarterback matchups are, well, interesting to varying degrees.
CJ Stroud has been a top-13 fantasy quarterback in three straight games and a top-five fantasy quarterback in two of his last three.
He now hosts a leaky Giants defense, one that has allowed three quarterbacks to log at least 26 fantasy points through six weeks.
Tanner McKee posted an abysmal -0.16 EPA per drop back in Week 6 with a 33% success rate. However, Jalen Hurts finished his short season with an -0.08 EPA per drop back and a 37% success rate.
No team is allowing a worse EPA per drop back on the season than the Cowboys.
After a Thursday Night Football win in Week 6, Bo Nix and the Broncos will have a few extra days to prepare for the Cardinals indoors. So far this season, Nix has not yet played in a top-half quarterback matchup, so this is a huge step up in ease for Nix.
Only one quarterback (Justin Herbert, 13.3 points) has been held under 16.5 points by the Cardinals this season.
As JJ pointed out in the Week 6 Data Dump on X, Nix's 4.9-yard average depth of target (aDOT) is 338th among 338 qualified quarterbacks since 2016 through Week 6.
Interestingly, though, the Cardinals are letting up a 10.3-yard aDOT, the highest in the NFL.
Baker Mayfield is slated to practice this week and could return against the Panthers, a top-five fantasy quarterback matchup.
Otherwise, Jalon Daniels has failed to hit 14 fantasy points in any of his three starts, including in his top-five matchup against the Cowboys in Week 5.
Matthew Stafford draws a matchup with a Raiders team that has held just one quarterback under 16.7 fantasy points (Justin Herbert had 7.9 against them).
Stafford, in three top-eight matchups, has averaged 23.7 fantasy points this season.
However, he'll be without Davante Adams for six to 10 weeks.
Geno Smith and the Jets are hosting the Dolphins, giving Smith a top-10 fantasy quarterback matchup.
Smith has only once been a top-15 weekly quarterback, Week 3 against the Lions, the league's friendliest matchup for quarterbacks.
That was actually Smith's only top-12 matchup this season.
Kyler Murray is firmly in the streaming conversation (52% rostered on Yahoo) against the Colts, who are a top-10 quarterback matchup both in passing and rushing points per game, after adjusting for opponents faced.
Minnesota is 14th in pressure rate allowed and now sees the league's weakest pass rush, as well.
As far as fantasy-relevant quarterbacks in rough spots, the most obvious name to dig into is Patrick Mahomes, who sees the Seahawks on the road in a game with a 42.5-point over/under.
Defensively, Seattle is top-two in EPA per drop back, adjusted passing yards per game, and adjusted fantasy points per game allowed to quarterbacks.
Mahomes has logged 21.7 and 10.5 fantasy points in two bottom-eight matchups so far this season.
Caleb Williams also gets a bottom-six fantasy matchup against the Patriots but is playing in a game with a 50.5-point over/under and low projected wind.
In his three (virtually) full games, Williams has finished as the QB1, QB27, and QB13.
WIDE RECEIVER MATCHUP NOTES
The top-five receiver matchups this week:
Packers (at Lions)
Giants (at Texans)
49ers (at Falcons)
Browns (at Titans)
Cowboys (at Eagles)
The Texans, surprisingly, remain a top-three adjusted wide receiver matchup on the season, but they're much more beatable on the perimeter (the fifth-best composite perimeter matchup) than in the slot (28th-best matchup).
That bodes well for both Malik Nabers and Malachi Fields.
With JJ McCarthy on the field in Week 6, Nabers and Fields each saw a 29% target share.
The Browns face a Titans team that allows the fifth-highest adjusted target share to receivers. They're middling against the slot (18th) but the second-best perimeter matchup for opposing wideouts, allowing a top-six target share, completion rate, and yards per route run to perimeter receivers.
This makes the matchup more enticing for Denzel Boston than for KC Concepcion, though it's still strong for Concepcion as a whole.
The Eagles have funneled volume and production inside, letting up 15.7 points per game to slot receivers, second-most in the league. They're 21st in points per game allowed to perimeter wide receivers by comparison.
CeeDee Lamb is much more likely to move into the slot than George Pickens (who has maxed out at an 11% slot share in a game this season).
Is this finally the week for Jaylen Waddle? The Broncos get a top-10 matchup with the Cardinals, and Waddle has had a team-high 22% target share on the season, a pretty average mark despite being the team leader.
Waddle and the Broncos could have big-play potential against a Cardinals team that allows a top-four aDOT to receivers specifically, but it's worth noting that he has had just 12 downfield targets (10-plus air yards) this season.
That ranks him 57th in the league. And it's not like other receivers are seeing those looks. Waddle leads with 12, and Sutton has 11. Nobody else on the team has more than four.
The top slot matchups (when accounting for volume and efficiency) this week belong to the Vikings (versus the Colts), Eagles (versus the Cowboys), Packers (at the Lions), Ravens (versus the Bengals), and Jets (versus the Dolphins).
In theory, this should help Jauan Jennings, but he has seen five targets all season.
Makai Lemon, Savion Williams, Zay Flowers, and Omar Cooper, though, could benefit.
Receivers who could benefit from a good perimeter matchup this week: Denzel Boston (at the Titans), Garrett Wilson (versus the Dolphins), Rome Odunze (versus the Patriots), Malik Nabers and Malachi Fields (at the Texans), AJ Brown (at the Bears), Jameson Williams (versus the Packers), and Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins (at the Ravens).
TIGHT END MATCHUP NOTES
The top tight end matchups this week:
Packers (at Lions)
Seahawks (vs. Chiefs)
Jets (vs. Dolphins)
Steelers (at Saints)
Broncos (at Cardinals)
AJ Barner has had target shares of 20%, 15%, 19%, and 9% in his last four games ahead of a meeting against a beatable Chiefs tight end defense.
Mason Taylor has run ahead of Kenyon Sadiq in two games since Taylor's return. Taylor has run 62% of the Jets' available routes compared to 29% for Sadiq in this split.
With injuries to wide receivers last week, Darnell Washington saw his highest route rate of the season (52%) behind Pat Freiermuth's 64% route rate. Washington out-targeted Freiermuth 6 to 3 (14% to 7%).
In fact, since Week 3, Washington has seen 18 targets to Freiermuth's 19.
Since finishing as the TE8 in Week 1, Evan Engram has been outside the top 15 in five straight games and is yet to see a 20% target share in a game this season.
Still, the Cardinals have allowed five different tight ends to log double-digit PPR points in 2026.
The Rams' trio of Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson, and Davis Allen set up well against the Raiders in a top-10 tight end matchup.
Without Davante Adams on the field in 2026, the Rams have run 13 personnel (three-tight end sets) on 86% of their plays.
In the last two games without Terrance Ferguson but with Parkinson, Higbee has a 12% target share with Parkinson at 9%; Higbee's route rate has been 70%, compared to 41% for Parkinson and 25% for Allen.
Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer get the worst possible tight end matchup in the league against the Rams, according to our adjusted fantasy points metric.
Los Angeles has not allowed a single tight end to get to 12.5 PPR points in a matchup, and that includes Trey McBride (12.3 points) and Isaiah Likely (8.3).
McBride and Likely each have 29% target shares, the same as Bowers, in active games.
Against the Rams specifically, McBride had a 27% share, and Likely was at 31%. The per-target efficiency has just been brutal for tight ends against the Rams.
McBride logged 53 receiving yards against them, the only tight end to go over 33 yards.
Travis Kelce meets the second-best tight end defense in the league, the Seahawks, and he does it on the road. McBride is also relevant for this conversation.
McBride did hang 18.1 PPR points on Seattle, but he needed a touchdown and eight catches to do it.
No tight end has had more than McBride's 41 receiving yards against Seattle this season.
I typically omit matchup notes for games we cover in the over/under section, but I'm looping in Kyle Pitts here for some connective tissue: Trey McBride.
Kyle Pitts' usage is on the upswing, but he now draws a bottom-six matchup with the 49ers.
McBride played the Niners, too, and he put up 16.5 PPR points (9 catches, 75 yards).
Only Chig Okonkwo (13.9 points) also went above 5.0 PPR points against the 49ers, who have yet to allow a tight end touchdown this season.
RUNNING BACK MATCHUP NOTES
The top running back matchups of the week:
Steelers (at Saints)
Jets (vs. Dolphins)
Lions (vs. Packers)
Buccaneers (at Panthers)
Falcons (vs. 49ers)
I hope you don't have to play against Jahmyr Gibbs or Bijan Robinson this week.
This past week, with Rico Dowdle back, Jaylen Warren led the Pittsburgh backfield with 9.8 PPR points to Dowdle's 7.9. That probably makes the gap seem a little closer than it was.
In terms of snap rate, Warren led overall (74% to 28%), on early downs (68% to 33%), and on third downs (93% to 7%). Warren also started 12 of 16 drives for the Steelers.
Notably, the Saints are letting up the most adjusted rushing fantasy points per game to backs but are 22nd in adjusted receiving points per game allowed to running backs.
Braelon Allen has played 94%, 86%, and 87% of the Jets' snaps in three games without Breece Hall. Hall could be back this week. Hall led roughly a 60/40 committee in the first two games of the season.
Either way, the Dolphins are a soft matchup for running backs despite ranking as the 24th-best matchup by yards before contact per attempt.
They're a top-seven matchup both by rushing and receiving fantasy points after adjusting for opponent.
Bucky Irving saw his snaps spike to a season-high 85% without Sean Tucker in Week 6, though Tucker hasn't cracked a 10% snap rate in any of his three games.
Even without Tucker, Kenny Gainwell played a season-low 15% snap rate in Week 6.
The Bucs are generating the second-most yards before contact per attempt, and the Panthers are 13th defensively, giving them the third-best yards before contact matchup on the board this week.
If Saquon Barkley (the subject of trade talks) misses again, then Dameon Pierce would — presumably — slot in as a featured back in a top-10 running back matchup. Last week, after Carson Steele left early, Pierce played every remaining snap over Jaydon Blue.
Pierce finished with a 97% snap rate and 15.0 PPR points (on 16.4 expected PPR points).
Dallas is allowing the fourth-most yards before contact per attempt in the league.
Jonathan Taylor's snap rates through Week 6: 89%, 95%, 85%, 71%, 74%, 81%. Seth McGowan is chipping away a bit here and there, and now JT faces the stone-cold worst matchup in the league for running backs — all while being a 6.5-point road underdog.
Minnesota has allowed two running backs to notch double-digit PPR points: D'Andre Swift (12.9) and Ollie Gordon (18.0). They're also the only two backs to surpass 60 scrimmage yards against Minnesota.
Tony Pollard hasn't had a top-15 PPR week all season and faces the Browns, who are the second-toughest running back matchup by our adjusted per-game numbers.
Either Tyrone Tracy or Cam Skattebo (gulp) could be in your plans this week after the Giants released Najee Harris, but they do hit the road to face a Texans team that has allowed the fewest adjusted rushing fantasy points per game to opposing running backs.
They've allowed the 11th-most receiving points, though, and the 9th-highest adjusted target share to running backs.
Zach Charbonnet is practicing this week and could play in Week 7 for a Seattle team that needs an answer at running back. The problem is that they face a Chiefs team that represents a bottom-three running back matchup.
However, they're more vulnerable through the air (16th-best receiving matchup for running backs) than on the ground (30th-best rushing matchup for running backs).
